The person responsible for a company's financial records, statutory tax compliance, payroll, and financial reporting — usually reporting to a Finance Manager, Controller, or MD.

Bookkeeping fundamentals

Six modules, in the order the money actually moves through the books.

MODULE 1

The accounting equation

Everything flows from this. Every transaction affects at least two accounts — that's double-entry.

Assets = Liabilities + Equity
MODULE 2

Debits & credits — the golden rules

Account typeIncreaseDecrease
AssetsDebitCredit
ExpensesDebitCredit
LiabilitiesCreditDebit
Equity / CapitalCreditDebit
Income / RevenueCreditDebit

Mnemonic — DEAD CLIC: Debit: Expenses, Assets, Drawings · Credit: Liabilities, Income, Capital.

MODULE 3

The journal — book of first entry

Every transaction is recorded as a journal entry before anything else.

Date Account Debit (Dr) Credit (Cr) -------------------------------------------------------- 01-Sep-26 Purchases 50,000 Cash 50,000 (Being cash purchase of stock)
Worked example — sell KES 100,000 of goods on credit, eTIMS invoice issued
Dr Accounts Receivable (Debtor) 100,000 Cr Sales Revenue 100,000
Worked example — pay KES 40,000 office rent via M-Pesa
Dr Rent Expense 40,000 Cr Bank/M-Pesa 40,000
MODULE 4

The general ledger — book of second entry

Groups all journal entries by account (T-accounts). Each account shows all its debits and credits and a running balance.

Sales Account --------------------------------------------- Date Details Amount | Date Details Amount | 01-Sep Debtor A/c 100,000 | 05-Sep Debtor B/c 60,000 --------------------------------------------- | Total 160,000

Process: post every entry's debit and credit to its ledger account, then balance each account at period end.

MODULE 5

The trial balance

All ledger balances at a point in time, checking total debits = total credits. It's a math check, not a guarantee of accuracy — it won't catch a transaction posted to the entirely wrong account.

If debits ≠ credits: trace a transposition, an omission, or a wrong-side posting.

MODULE 6

From trial balance to financial statements

Trial Balance→ Adjusting entries

Kenya-specific systems

The statutory infrastructure every entry eventually has to pass through.

eTIMS

KRA's mandatory e-invoicing system, replacing the old ETR/TIMS devices. Every business — VAT-registered or not, with limited exceptions — must generate invoices through it, via web portal, mobile app, or an API/ERP integration.

Each invoice gets a KRA-validated invoice number and QR code. Only expenses backed by an eTIMS invoice are tax-deductible for the paying business — this is why accountants chase suppliers for eTIMS-compliant invoices.

iTax

KRA's online portal for filing returns — VAT, PAYE, Corporation Tax, WHT, TOT — paying taxes, applying for tax compliance certificates, and managing PIN/KYC details.

Invoice vs. bill

Invoice — you issue it to a customer. Money owed to you. Accounts Receivable.

Bill — you receive it from a supplier. Money owed by you. Accounts Payable.

Both should be matched to eTIMS records for tax compliance.

Monthly workflow, tied together

  • Issue eTIMS invoices for sales → Dr Debtor/Bank, Cr Sales
  • Record supplier bills → Dr Expense/Purchases, Cr Creditor
  • Post all entries to the general ledger
  • Reconcile bank and M-Pesa statements
  • Extract trial balance
  • Compute VAT, PAYE, and other statutory dues
  • File on iTax and pay by deadlines
  • Prepare management accounts

Daily and weekly study routine

  • Daily, 1 hour: 10 minutes recalling yesterday's formula from memory, 25 minutes learning one new function or feature, 25 minutes applying it to a mini-project.
  • Weekend, 2 hours: one project block — build something end to end, even if small.
  • End of every week: write three lines on what you learned and one thing that confused you; search for that one thing next week.
  • End of every month: clean up one project and keep it as a portfolio piece.
  • Pace: about three months gets most people to basic analyst-level skills. Prior spreadsheet experience shortens that; a busy job lengthens it.

PROJECT 4

Digital clock

Excel only recalculates when something changes, so a ticking clock needs a small VBA macro that re-writes the time every second.

NOW()VBAApplication.OnTimeCustom number format
LIVE DEMO — WHAT YOUR SHEET WILL SHOW
--:--:--
Build it in Excel
  1. Name a sheet Clock. Merge a few cells (for example B2:F4), set a large bold font and a dark fill.
  2. Format the merged cell as Custom → hh:mm:ss AM/PM.
  3. Press Alt+F11, insert a Module and paste the code below.
  4. Run StartClock. Add a button (Developer → Insert → Button) for Start and Stop.
  5. Save as .xlsm (macro-enabled). To auto-start, call StartClock from Workbook_Open in ThisWorkbook.
Dim NextTick As Date

Sub StartClock()
    Sheets("Clock").Range("B2").Value = Now
    NextTick = Now + TimeValue("00:00:01")
    Application.OnTime NextTick, "StartClock"
End Sub

Sub StopClock()
    On Error Resume Next
    Application.OnTime NextTick, "StartClock", , False
End Sub

' In ThisWorkbook:
Private Sub Workbook_Open()
    StartClock
End Sub

Private Sub Workbook_BeforeClose(Cancel As Boolean)
    StopClock
End Sub

Automated entry form for voluminous data

Excel Data FormVBA UserFormListObjectsValidation
Build it in Excel
  1. Quickest option — built-in Data Form: File → Options → Quick Access Toolbar → "All Commands" → add Form…. Click inside your Table and press the Form button.
  2. Custom option — UserForm: Alt+F11 → Insert → UserForm. Add text boxes (txtName, txtAmount), a combo box (cboCategory) and a Submit button (btnSubmit).

Private Sub btnSubmit_Click()
    Dim tbl As ListObject, r As ListRow

    If Trim(Me.txtName.Value) = "" Then
        MsgBox "Enter a name.", vbExclamation: Exit Sub
    End If
    If Not IsNumeric(Me.txtAmount.Value) Or Val(Me.txtAmount.Value) <= 0 Then
        MsgBox "Amount must be a positive number.", vbExclamation: Exit Sub
    End If

    Set tbl = ThisWorkbook.Sheets("Data").ListObjects("tblEntries")
    Set r = tbl.ListRows.Add
    r.Range(1, 1).Value = Date
    r.Range(1, 2).Value = Trim(Me.txtName.Value)
    r.Range(1, 3).Value = Me.cboCategory.Value
    r.Range(1, 4).Value = CDbl(Me.txtAmount.Value)

    Me.txtName.Value = "": Me.txtAmount.Value = ""
    Me.txtName.SetFocus
End Sub

LET and LAMBDA — readable, reusable formulas

LET gives names to intermediate results so long formulas become readable. LAMBDA turns a formula into your own function.

PAYE-style calculation using LET (example bands, same as the Taxes tab)
=LET(
    gross,   B2,
    nssf,    MIN(gross*6%, 4320),
    taxable, gross - nssf,
    paye,    SUMPRODUCT((taxable-{0;24000;32333})*(taxable>{0;24000;32333})*{0.10;0.15;0.05}) - 2400,
    MAX(paye, 0)
)
Make it a reusable function: Formulas → Name Manager → New. Name: PAYE_EX. Refers to:
=LAMBDA(taxable,
    MAX(SUMPRODUCT((taxable-{0;24000;32333})*(taxable>{0;24000;32333})*{0.10;0.15;0.05}) - 2400, 0)
)

' then in any cell:
=PAYE_EX(D2)

Advanced lookups

' Two-way lookup (row and column)
=INDEX(C2:H20, MATCH(A25, B2:B20, 0), MATCH(B25, C1:H1, 0))

' XLOOKUP with two criteria
=XLOOKUP(1, (tblA[Customer]=A2) * (tblA[Month]=B2), tblA[Amount], 0)

' Approximate match for banded rates (bands sorted ascending)
=XLOOKUP(B2, bands[From], bands[Rate], , -1)

' Wildcard match: any supplier name containing "Safari"
=XLOOKUP("*Safari*", tblSuppliers[Name], tblSuppliers[PIN], "Not found", 2)

' Sum across a condition on a calculated array
=SUMPRODUCT((MONTH(tblSales[Date])=9) * (tblSales[Branch]="Nairobi") * tblSales[Amount])

PivotTable mastery

  1. Start from a Table (Ctrl+T) so the Pivot grows with your data; Insert → PivotTable.
  2. Drag Branch to Rows, Month to Columns and Amount to Values. Change Value Field Settings from Sum to Count or Average when needed.
  3. Right-click a date → Group to roll days up into months, quarters and years.
  4. Value Field Settings → Show Values As → % of Column Total, or Difference From previous month, to analyse mix and growth.
  5. Add Slicers and a Timeline (PivotTable Analyze) so anyone can filter by branch or period with one click, and connect them to several Pivots.
  6. Add a calculated field (for example Margin = Sales − Cost) and a PivotChart for the dashboard.
  7. Tick "Add this data to the Data Model" to pivot across several tables, and use GETPIVOTDATA to pull Pivot numbers into formatted reports.

Power Query — clean data once, refresh forever

Data → Get Data brings in Excel files, CSVs, folders, PDFs and web pages. Every step you click is recorded and replays on Refresh, so next month's messy export cleans itself.

  • Everyday steps: remove columns, change data types, Trim and Clean text, split columns, fill down, replace values, filter rows.
  • Reshape: Unpivot Columns turns month-per-column reports into tidy rows ready for a PivotTable.
  • Combine: Append stacks tables (twelve monthly files into one); Merge joins tables like a database JOIN, replacing VLOOKUP at scale.
  • Folder import: point at a folder of statements and Combine & Transform loads every file, including next month's.
let
    Source  = Excel.CurrentWorkbook(){[Name="tblSales"]}[Content],
    Typed   = Table.TransformColumnTypes(Source,
                 {{"Date", type date}, {"Amount", type number}}),
    NoBlank = Table.SelectRows(Typed, each [Amount] <> null),
    Trimmed = Table.TransformColumns(NoBlank, {{"Customer", Text.Trim, type text}}),
    Grouped = Table.Group(Trimmed, {"Branch"},
                 {{"Total", each List.Sum([Amount]), type number}})
in
    Grouped

Power Pivot and DAX — a data model with measures

Load tables into the Data Model, relate them (Sales → Calendar, Sales → Customers) and write measures that recalculate for every filter.

Total Sales   := SUM(Sales[Amount])
Total Cost    := SUM(Sales[Cost])
Margin %      := DIVIDE([Total Sales] - [Total Cost], [Total Sales])
Sales YTD     := TOTALYTD([Total Sales], 'Calendar'[Date])
Sales LY      := CALCULATE([Total Sales], SAMEPERIODLASTYEAR('Calendar'[Date]))
Growth %      := DIVIDE([Total Sales] - [Sales LY], [Sales LY])
Top Customers := CALCULATE([Total Sales], TOPN(5, ALL(Customers[Name]), [Total Sales]))

13-week cash-flow forecast layout

  • Rows: opening cash, receipts (customers, other), payments (suppliers, payroll, PAYE/VAT/NSSF dates, rent, loan instalments), net movement, closing cash.
  • Columns: week-ending dates generated with =B1+7.
  • Pull receipts and payments from your invoice and bill ageing using SUMIFS on due dates, then flag any week where closing cash falls below your minimum.

Model design and dashboard rules

  • One direction of flow: Inputs → Calculations → Outputs, on separate sheets or clearly separated blocks.
  • Colour code: blue font for inputs, black for formulas, green for links to other sheets.
  • No hard-coded numbers inside formulas. Put rates and thresholds in labelled cells; tax rates change every Finance Act.
  • Use Tables and structured references (tblSales[Amount]) so ranges never go stale.
  • Check cells: debits = credits, balance sheet balances, subtotals agree to totals, each shown as a TRUE/FALSE check.
  • Avoid volatile functions (OFFSET, INDIRECT, TODAY in thousands of cells) in big models; they slow recalculation.
  • Dashboards: three to five KPI cards on top, one trend chart, one breakdown chart, slicers on the side; every chart answers one question and has a clear title.
  • Protect and document: a "Read me" sheet with purpose, owner, date and assumptions; lock formula cells.

Before you pay for any course

Promotional course ads promise a lot. A paid course can be worth it for structure and a certificate, but check these first:

  • Is there a detailed syllabus and a free sample lesson?
  • Who teaches it, and can you find their work or reviews outside the course website?
  • Do you receive the practice files and finished projects?
  • Does the certificate carry weight with employers, or is it only a completion record?
  • Is there a refund policy, and is the "promo price" a genuine reduction?
  • Compare with free material (see Resources). Everything in this site's Analyst Path can be learned for free.

A first SQL query to aim for
SELECT branch,
       SUM(amount)                              AS total_sales,
       COUNT(*)                                 AS invoices,
       RANK() OVER (ORDER BY SUM(amount) DESC)  AS branch_rank
FROM   sales
WHERE  sale_date >= '2026-01-01'
GROUP  BY branch
HAVING SUM(amount) > 100000
ORDER  BY total_sales DESC;

Build a portfolio of 3–5 projects

Employers hire on evidence. Publish each project with a short write-up: the problem, the data, what you did, what you found.

  • Sales dashboard — PivotTables, slicers and KPIs for a retail or distribution business.
  • Loan tracker — Project 6 extended with arrears ageing and a collections summary.
  • Budget-vs-actual model — a department budget with variance flags and a forecast.
  • Payroll workbook — gross-to-net with PAYE, NSSF, SHIF and Housing Levy, using current-year rates.
  • Reconciliation tool — match supplier bills from KRA iTax against your books and list what is missing.
  • One public-dataset analysis — Kaggle or a government open-data set, with a Power BI report on top.

Use dummy or anonymised data for anything that comes from a real employer or client.

Becoming a Microsoft Excel instructor

Master the topic→ Teach colleagues→ Record short tutorials→ Write a curriculum→ Run paid cohorts→ Sell templates
  • Teach to learn: explaining a formula to someone else exposes every gap in your own understanding.
  • Start small: a 30-minute lunchtime session for colleagues, then 5-minute screen recordings on one function each.
  • Add credibility: a MOS certification and a portfolio of finished projects.
  • Where to teach: in-house staff training, weekend classes, corporate workshops, colleges, online platforms.
  • Teaching method: demonstrate, let learners copy, then give a slightly different task so they apply it.
